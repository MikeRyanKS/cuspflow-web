Most comparisons of dental clinic management software for Ghana are feature grids, and feature grids do not tell you the four things that will actually bite: whether amounts print properly in cedis, how the software handles tax after the January 2026 change, whether the vendor expects you to register as a data controller, and whether a second branch costs you a second system.

![From a cedi payment at the front desk to a numbered receipt to a branch total covering Accra and Kumasi](/blog/dental-clinic-management-software-ghana/ghana-clinic-receipt-to-branch-report.svg "The branch has to be stamped on the payment as it is taken, because nobody can reconstruct it a week later.")

## Does it work in cedis, on the printer you own?

Make them show you, on screen and then on paper.

Ask for an ordinary invoice: a scale and polish, a composite filling, and whatever you sell over the counter. Check the separator, the currency label and the decimal places. Then send it to the printer you actually have, which in most Ghanaian clinics is an 80mm thermal roll at reception and A4 in the office. Software that only lays out for letter-size paper means either new hardware or a receptionist cutting pages by hand.

One more thing worth asking about the receipt: can you set the header and footer yourself? Clinic name, location, phone number, a line about the refund policy. It sounds cosmetic until a patient takes the slip to their insurer.

## How should tax appear after the 2026 change?

As a line you control, and sometimes as no line at all.

The VAT Act, 2025 took effect on 1 January 2026 and replaced the old layered calculation. The standard rate is still 15%, the NHIL and GETFund levies remain at 2.5% each, and the COVID-19 Health Recovery Levy was repealed, which brings the effective rate to 20% instead of the 21.9% the old stacking produced. Health services are exempt by law, so a clinic billing only treatment usually issues no tax invoice at all, while the products you retail can sit differently. Check your own position with the [Ghana Revenue Authority](https://gra.gov.gh) or your accountant rather than with a software vendor.

What that means for a buying decision is narrow. You need a system that lets you set a rate, label the line, and print a clean receipt with the tax line switched off. Ask too whether the product connects to the GRA's electronic VAT invoicing, which has been rolled out in phases to VAT-registered taxpayers. Ours does not, and very few clinic systems do. A straight no you can plan around; vagueness you cannot.

## Can it run Accra and Kumasi on one system?

Only if every record is stamped with the branch it happened at, as it happens.

This is the question that separates products built for one site from products built for a group. A patient who normally attends Accra but turns up in Kumasi with toothache should keep one record, one balance and one treatment history, while the payment, the chair and the stock movement all belong to Kumasi. If the software works out the branch afterwards from the patient's registration details, your branch reports are fiction.

## Do you have to register as a data controller?

Probably, and it is worth knowing before you choose where patient records live.

Ghana's Data Protection Act, 2012 requires a data controller to register with the Commission before processing personal data, and the [Data Protection Commission](https://dpc.gov.gh/registration/) runs the portal and the public register. Your clinic is the controller. The vendor processes on your behalf, so ask them to say in writing that the records are yours and to tell you who on their side can read them.

## Common questions

### What about a patient who pays by mobile money?

Look for mobile money as a recorded payment method with a provider list you maintain, so the payment reconciles against a named cashier at the end of the day. Ours records it that way rather than connecting to any provider directly.

### How long does moving off paper take?

Less than clinics expect for appointments and prices, longer for history. Our advice, and the Uganda version of [this same buying exercise](/blog/dental-clinic-software-uganda) says the same, is to import patients and balances first and leave old treatment notes on paper.
