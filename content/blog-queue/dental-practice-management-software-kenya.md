Dental practice management software for Kenya gets judged on four things that have nothing to do with the feature grid: whether it works properly in Kenyan shillings, whether VAT at 16% appears on the receipt as its own line, what the vendor says about eTIMS when you ask them directly, and whether you can get your own records back out. A product can be excellent at charting and still fail all four.

![A Kenyan dental receipt flow: treatment billed in shillings, VAT at sixteen percent added as its own line, then a receipt the patient keeps](/blog/dental-practice-management-software-kenya/kenya-dental-receipt-vat-flow.svg "The receipt is the only part of your system most patients ever see.")

## What should you ask about eTIMS?

Ask what the software does, and then ask what is left for you to do. The gap between those two answers is the real answer.

The Electronic Tax Invoice Regulations extended the obligation to issue electronic tax invoices to businesses carrying on business in Kenya, and the [Kenya Revenue Authority](https://kra.go.ke) is the place to confirm what currently applies to a clinic of your size and registration status. What matters commercially is that very few clinic systems, ours included, connect to eTIMS directly. Most clinics generate their tax invoices through KRA's own tools and use their clinic software for everything else.

A vendor who says "we handle eTIMS" should be asked to show you, on screen, on a live invoice. A vendor who says "we do not connect to it, here is where the handover sits" is giving you something you can plan around. Vagueness is the answer to watch out for, because you are the one who will be standing in front of the tax authority, not them.

## Does it genuinely work in shillings?

Test it rather than trust it, and the test takes a minute.

Ask for an invoice on screen for something ordinary, a filling and a scale and polish. Check that the figure reads the way your receptionist would write it, with the thousands separator in the right place and the currency label the clinic uses. Then check the printed version, because some systems format the screen correctly and the receipt in a US style.

Printing is worth its own question. Most Kenyan clinics run a thermal roll at the desk and A4 in the office. Software that assumes letter-size paper will cost you a printer or a workaround.

## How hard is it to leave?

Harder than any vendor will volunteer, so ask at the point when they most want your business.

Two questions settle it. Can you export every record, including treatment history and payments, without asking anyone's permission or paying for it? And if you decide to move, is the import tool at the other end something you can run yourself?

That second one cuts both ways, which is why we built [a free CSV import with a validation dry run and a rollback window](/docs/getting-started/importing-your-data) rather than a paid migration service. If moving in is easy, moving out is too, and that is the honest trade. A vendor whose answer to "how do I leave" is a quote has told you something about the relationship.

## Common questions

### Will it work with an unreliable connection?

Test it on your own line at your busiest hour, not in the vendor's office. Ours is online only with no offline mode, which avoids sync problems between machines but does mean a dropped connection stops work at the desk.

### Can we run Nairobi and Mombasa on one system?

You should be able to, with per-branch stock, chairs and schedules, and without a per-location charge. On [our plans](/pricing) branches are unlimited at every tier, so a second site changes your workload and not your bill.

### Do patients' insurance arrangements fit?

Most Kenyan clinics deal with private carriers rather than a single national payer, so look for a system where you maintain your own carrier list and track each claim's status. A product designed around one country's national insurance scheme will fight you.
