Most advice on opening a second dental clinic is about money: the loan, the lease, the fit-out. The checklist that decides whether it actually works is about systems. You need to be able to leave the first clinic for a week. Every payment needs to be stamped with a branch already. And one patient has to exist once across both sites, not twice.

![A two column checklist of what to settle before signing a lease and what to settle before opening the doors](/blog/opening-a-second-dental-clinic-checklist/second-clinic-readiness-checks.svg "The left column takes months and the right column takes an afternoon, which is why the right column is the one that gets skipped.")

## What has to be true at the first clinic?

Four things, and none of them appear on a bank's application form.

The chairs at the existing site have to be genuinely full, not busy on Saturdays. A second site built to absorb overflow that does not exist is just a second rent bill. Then: you have to be able to disappear for a week without the day falling apart, which means a named person already opens and closes and signs off the till, rather than you doing it by habit. Our [receptionist's opening and closing routine](/blog/dental-receptionist-daily-checklist) is a reasonable test of whether that person exists yet.

Third, the price list has to be written down rather than recalled. Two sites quoting different figures for the same filling is the fastest way to lose a patient's trust, and it happens within weeks. Fourth, you need to know what the first clinic earns and collects month by month, because that is the only honest basis for guessing at the second.

## What breaks on the first day of two sites?

The things that were ambiguous when there was only one place for them to happen.

Payments are the big one. Every payment, appointment, chair booking and stock movement has to carry the branch it happened at, recorded as it happens. Software that works the branch out afterwards from where the patient was first registered will put a Kigali payment in the Kampala column forever, and you will not notice until the annual accounts. A patient can walk into either site, so their registration branch tells you nothing about where today's money came from.

Stock is the second. Two sites sharing one pooled stock figure leaves both reorder levels wrong, so count and reorder per branch from the start. Then receipt numbering, which has to be gap-free and unable to clash between sites. And staff who cover both sites need two separate facts held about them: the branch they are employed at, which payroll uses, and the branch they are working at today, which the schedule uses. Systems with a single field for that will misreport one of the two.

## Which numbers do you need per branch?

Revenue, collections, outstanding balance and chair utilisation, each one split by site and never only consolidated.

A single clinic-wide total is actively misleading once you have two sites, because a strong first clinic will carry a failing second one for most of a year before the combined figure moves enough to alarm you. Look at them separately and weekly. Chair utilisation is the earliest warning of the four: a second site with empty chairs at eleven in the morning has a marketing problem, while one with full chairs and poor collections has a front-desk problem, and those need opposite responses.

## Common questions

### Should both sites use the same price list?

Yes, unless rent or local incomes genuinely differ, in which case set the second site's prices deliberately and write down why. What you want to avoid is two lists that drifted apart because nobody owned either of them.

### Who should run the second site on the first day?

Somebody who has already worked at the first one. A new clinic with an entirely new team has nobody who knows how your day is supposed to feel, and the habits you spent years building do not transfer by document. Moving one experienced person across and backfilling behind them is slower and it works.
