Three things decide whether dental clinic software for Rwanda is worth your money, and none of them sits on the feature list. Your tax invoice has to come from a certified electronic billing machine, not from the software. Amounts have to total and print cleanly in francs. And your clinic, not the vendor, carries the registration duty for patient data.

![A two column split of what a clinic system handles and what an RRA-certified electronic billing machine handles](/blog/dental-clinic-software-rwanda/ebm-and-clinic-system-split.svg "The right-hand column is not a gap in your software. It is a separate legal obligation with its own device behind it.")

## What does the software do about EBM invoicing?

It should stay out of the way, and say so plainly.

Rwanda requires a person carrying out a taxable activity to issue invoices through an invoicing system certified by the revenue authority. The [RRA's own description](https://www.rra.gov.rw/fileadmin/img/electronic-billing-machines.html) sets out the two parts of an electronic billing machine: a certified invoicing system, and a sales data controller that passes the sales data on. Your clinic system is neither of those, ours included.

That is not a small point to leave until after you have signed. It decides what reception physically does for each paying patient, how many pieces of paper they hand over, and which of the two records your accountant works from at month end. A vendor who answers this one with "we handle all your invoicing" is either confused about Rwanda or hoping you are. Ask instead whether anything in their product has been certified, and expect the answer to be no.

## Will it handle Rwandan francs without inventing cents?

Check it on screen, in a real amount, and then on paper.

Francs are quoted in whole units, so a system that insists on two decimal places leaves every figure in your clinic trailing a meaningless ",00". Totals also run long: a denture case clears six figures on its own. Look at the thousands separator, the currency label and the rounding on a part payment, because a system that rounds an instalment badly will leave a patient owing one franc forever and your receptionist unable to close the balance. Our notes on [taking a payment at checkout](/docs/billing-payments/checkout-and-taking-payment) cover how part payments and change are meant to behave.

Printing is the other half. Most clinics in Kigali run an 80mm thermal roll at the desk and A4 in the back office, and software that only lays out for one of those is a hardware purchase you have not budgeted for.

## Who has to register under the data protection law?

You do, as the controller, and the vendor registers separately as a processor.

![The CuspFlow patient record, with history, contact details and balance in one place](/screenshots/patients.webp "Access is set per role, so a Receptionist can book and bill without reading a clinical note.")

Law No. 058/2021, published in October 2021, requires controllers and processors to register with the National Cyber Security Authority, and it treats storing personal data outside the country as something your registration has to cover. For a cloud product that is the question to put in writing: where do the records sit, and does our registration need to say so. Then ask who on the vendor's side can open a record at all. On our side access is governed by the eight [staff roles and permissions](/docs/core-concepts/roles-and-permissions), which is the same mechanism that stops your own front desk reading a payroll figure.

## Common questions

### Can one system cover Kigali and a second site?

Yes, provided each payment, chair booking and stock movement is stamped with the branch as it happens rather than guessed from the patient's registration details afterwards. The [Kenya edition of these checks](/blog/dental-practice-management-software-kenya) goes through the same test in more detail.

### What happens when the connection at reception drops?

You stop, which is the honest answer for any cloud product. What you can reasonably ask is how much work is lost: a system that holds a half-finished treatment plan until the line returns is different from one that throws the form away.
