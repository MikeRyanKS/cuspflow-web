Dental clinic software in Uganda has to handle four local realities before any feature list matters: prices in Ugandan shillings, VAT at 18% shown on the receipt, printing to whatever paper the clinic actually owns, and payments that arrive as cash or mobile money far more often than card. Plenty of products sold into Uganda were designed for somewhere else, and it shows within a week.

## Does it work in shillings, or only display them?

There is a difference, and you can spot it in a demo. Ask the salesperson to show you an invoice for a crown. If the figure comes back as 1,250,000 with the separators in the right places and the currency label the clinic recognises, the product was built to be configured per clinic. If it comes back in dollars with a conversion note, or with a decimal comma where you expect a comma for thousands, the number formatting is hard-coded and you will be fighting it at every checkout.

## What does it do about VAT and receipts?

Two separate questions, and vendors tend to answer only the first.

VAT should be a setting you control, charged at your rate and printed as its own line on the receipt so a patient can see what they paid tax on. If your clinic is not VAT registered, you should be able to switch it off without the figures breaking.

Receipt printing is where Ugandan clinics get caught out. Most have a thermal roll printer at the desk and an A4 printer in the back office, and the software needs to handle both. Being able to [set your own paper size, header and footer](/docs/billing-payments/customizing-receipts-and-printing) matters more than it sounds, because a receipt is the only piece of your clinic most patients take home.

Then ask the question that separates a confident vendor from a vague one: what does this do about EFRIS, and what is still left for me to do by hand? The [Uganda Revenue Authority](https://ura.go.ug) requires VAT-registered businesses and certain gazetted sectors to issue fiscal invoices and receipts through it. Very few clinic systems connect to EFRIS directly, and a vendor who implies otherwise without showing you is worth a second look. An honest answer is useful. A confident wrong one costs you later.

## How does it record cash and mobile money?

Mobile money is not an edge case in Uganda, so a system that offers only cash and card is asking your receptionist to lie about how the money arrived. Look for payment methods that match reality: cash, card, mobile money, bank transfer and insurance, with your own list of mobile money providers.

Be precise about what that means, though. In most clinic systems, ours included, mobile money is a method you record rather than a wallet the software collects from. The patient pays on their phone, the receptionist marks it as mobile money, and the figure lands in the day's takings under the right heading. That distinction matters when one vendor's claim starts to sound bigger than another's.

Because every payment carries the method and the cashier who took it, the end-of-day count becomes a subtraction rather than an argument.

## Common questions

### Does it work on a slow connection?

Cloud systems vary, so test it on your own line rather than the vendor's. Ours is online only, with no offline mode, which is a genuine trade-off: it means no sync conflicts between machines, but it also means you need a working connection to use it.

### Can one clinic run more than one branch?

It should, without paying extra for the privilege. If a vendor charges per location, a second branch in Entebbe or Gulu suddenly has a price attached. On [our plans](/pricing), branches are unlimited at every tier.

### How do we move our existing records in?

Ask to see the import tool, not a slide about it. A free, built-in CSV import with a dry run, which tells you what would fail before anything is written, is the difference between a weekend and a month.
