Growing from one clinic to several is a milestone, but it introduces a new class of problems. Suddenly you are juggling separate schedules, separate stock, staff who cover more than one location, and an owner who needs to see the whole group at once. Software that treats each branch as an afterthought makes this painful. Software built for it makes growth feel effortless.

## The two things multi-branch software must get right

Multi-location management comes down to a balance between separation and roll-up.

### Clean separation per branch

Each branch needs its own reality: its own chairs, its own daily schedule, its own stock levels, and its own waiting room. A patient checked in at your downtown branch should never appear on the board at your uptown branch. Getting this wrong creates confusion at the front desk and errors in the numbers.

### Group-level roll-up for owners

At the same time, the owner needs to see across every location: total revenue, outstanding balances, patient counts and staff performance for the whole group. The system should give you both the branch view and the group view without exporting spreadsheets and stitching them together by hand.

CuspFlow is built around exactly this split. [Multi-branch management](/features) keeps each location's operations isolated while rolling financial and operational data up to the group.

## Staff who work across locations

Real clinics are not tidy. A dentist might work Mondays at one branch and Wednesdays at another. A receptionist might cover a sick colleague across town. Your system needs to handle a staff member's home branch as an HR fact while letting them actually work wherever they are needed that day, with attendance and schedules following them.

This distinction matters more than it sounds. If "which branch does this person belong to" and "which branch are they working today" are the same field, covering shifts quietly corrupts your records. Good multi-branch software keeps them separate.

## One login, one bill, one source of truth

Owners of growing groups do not want a separate login, a separate subscription and a separate support relationship for each clinic. The healthiest model is one account that spans every branch, one subscription, and unlimited branches included rather than billed per location.

That last point is worth repeating: if your software charges again for each new branch, it is taxing your growth. Look for [pricing](/pricing) where branches are unlimited on every plan and you only pay for staff seats and storage.

## Reporting that respects the structure

When you run several clinics, your reports need a branch filter on everything: revenue, appointments, inventory, staff performance. You should be able to answer "how did the uptown branch do last month" and "how did the whole group do" from the same screen. Being able to export any of it keeps your numbers portable.
