The fear of losing patient records keeps many clinics stuck on software they have outgrown. It is a reasonable fear. Years of history, appointments and billing are not something you gamble with. But switching is far safer than most owners expect when you follow a clear process and use a tool built for it.

## Before you migrate: get your data out

Whatever system you are on now, your first step is to export your data. A trustworthy vendor lets you export everything in a portable format such as CSV. If your current provider makes this hard, that is itself a good reason to leave, but push until you have clean exports of:

- Patients and their contact and medical details
- Families or household groupings
- Appointments and history
- Staff and their roles
- Inventory and price lists

Keep these exports safe. They are your safety net for the entire move.

## Migrate in dependency order

Data has a natural order. Some records depend on others existing first. Import them in this sequence to avoid broken links:

1. Branches and chairs
2. Staff
3. Patients and families
4. Appointments
5. Price catalog and inventory

A good [data import tool](/features) understands this order and guides you through it, rather than letting you import appointments for patients who do not exist yet.

## Insist on a validation dry-run

The most important feature in any migration tool is a dry-run that checks every row before anything is saved. It should tell you, up front, which rows are valid, which have warnings, and which have errors, so you can fix problems in your spreadsheet and re-upload. Nothing should be written to your live clinic until the check passes.

This is the difference between a calm migration and a frightening one. When errors are caught before import, a mistake costs you a few minutes of editing, not a corrupted patient list.

## Column mapping should be automatic

Your old system's column names will not match the new one exactly. Look for import tools that auto-map columns for you and let you confirm or adjust, so you are not hand-matching dozens of fields. CuspFlow's importer fuzzy-matches your headers and shows you the mapping before you commit.

## Run the two systems in parallel briefly

You do not have to switch everything in one dramatic moment. Import your data, then run the new system alongside the old one for a short overlap. Check a handful of real patient records against the originals. Book a few test appointments. Once your team is confident, make the new system the source of truth.

## Watch for hidden migration fees

Some vendors charge to bring your data in, or hold it hostage when you try to leave. Neither is acceptable. Your data belongs to your clinic. The whole point of a [free, built-in import tool](/features) and free export is that switching costs you time and care, not money.
