Dental clinic software in Nigeria is best judged on four things that sit outside the feature list: whether money reads correctly in naira on a printed receipt, whether you control how tax appears, who the vendor says is accountable for your patient records, and whether you can get everything back out yourself. A system can chart teeth beautifully and still fail all four.

![Four checks before buying dental clinic software in Nigeria: naira on the printout, how tax is shown, who holds the records, and export](/blog/dental-clinic-software-nigeria/nigeria-clinic-software-four-checks.svg "Each of the four is something you can verify in a demo, in about ten minutes, if you insist on seeing it on screen.")

## Does it genuinely work in naira?

Test it rather than ask, because every vendor says yes to this question.

Have them put a real invoice on screen: a filling, a scale and polish, and a tube of something you actually sell. Check that the figure reads the way your receptionist writes it, with the separator in the right place and the currency label your clinic uses. Then print it. Some systems format the screen correctly and send a US-style number to the printer, which nobody notices until a patient queries their receipt.

Printing deserves its own question. Most Nigerian clinics run an 80mm thermal roll at the desk and A4 in the back office, so a product that only knows letter-size paper will cost you either a printer or a workaround.

## How should tax appear on the receipt?

As a line you control, including the case where there should be no line at all.

Nigeria's standard VAT rate is 7.5%, and the tax reform that took effect on 1 January 2026 lists healthcare services among its exemptions. Whether that covers everything your clinic bills is a question for your own accountant, not for a software salesperson. What you need from the software is the ability to set the rate, label the line, and produce a clean receipt when the line is switched off entirely. Plenty of systems assume tax always applies and leave an awkward 0.00 row on the page.

Ask directly whether the product connects to any tax authority system. Ours does not, and most do not. Clinics in Kenya hit the same wall with electronic invoicing, which [the Kenya buying guide](/blog/dental-practice-management-software-kenya) goes into. A vendor who is vague here is leaving you to discover the gap later.

## Who is accountable for the patient records?

Your clinic is, and no software contract changes that.

Under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 your practice is the data controller and the vendor processes data on your behalf. Organisations that meet the Commission's threshold register as controllers of major importance, and the [Nigeria Data Protection Commission](https://ndpc.gov.ng) publishes the current guidance on who that captures. Ask the vendor to state in writing that the records are yours, where they are held, and who on their side can read them.

Then ask what the software does about it internally. Role separation is the part clinics skip: a Receptionist taking cash should not be able to open payroll, and a Doctor should not need Finance rights to write a treatment note. If every user logs in as the owner, you have no record of who did what.
