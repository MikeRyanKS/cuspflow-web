Before you compare features, check four things in any dental clinic software for Tanzania: whether it totals and prints in shillings properly, what it expects you to do about your EFD, whether a second branch is stamped on every payment as the money is taken, and who registers with the Personal Data Protection Commission. A feature grid answers none of those.

## How does it sit alongside your EFD?

It does not replace it, and a vendor who implies otherwise has just told you something useful about the rest of their claims.

A VAT-registered trader is required to have an electronic fiscal device, and the revenue authority also accepts a virtual fiscal device in its place, so the receipt the state recognises comes out of that device rather than out of your clinic system. Thresholds for traders below the VAT line have moved more than once, so check your own position with the [TRA](https://www.tra.go.tz) or your accountant instead of with a software salesperson.

Your clinic system is there for everything the fiscal receipt does not hold. Which teeth were treated. What the price was before the discount. Who took the money, at which branch, on which chair. Ours does not connect to an EFD, and very few clinic systems anywhere do. So reception issues the fiscal receipt from the device and the clinic slip from the system, and somebody has to make the two totals agree before going home. Ask about that in the demo and write down the answer, because a straight no you can plan around.

## Does it genuinely total in Tanzanian shillings?

Ask for a real invoice on screen, then send it to the printer you already own.

Shilling amounts run long. A crown and two fillings puts you well into six figures before anything else is added, which makes the thousands separator and the absence of trailing decimals matter far more than they would in a currency with cents. Check the currency label too, since plenty of systems quietly fall back to a dollar sign for anything they do not recognise.

Then print it. Most Tanzanian clinics have an 80mm thermal roll at reception and A4 in the office, and software laid out only for letter-size paper means either new hardware or a receptionist trimming pages by hand. Ask whether you can set the header and footer yourself: clinic name, street, phone number, a line on the refund policy. ## Can it run Dar es Salaam and Arusha as one clinic?

Only if the branch is stamped on each payment at the moment it is taken.

A patient who normally attends Dar es Salaam and turns up in Arusha with toothache should keep one record, one balance and one history, while the payment, the chair and the stock movement all belong to Arusha. Where a system works the branch out afterwards from the patient's registration details, your branch figures are fiction and you will not find out for months. ## Common questions

### Do we have to register with the Personal Data Protection Commission?

Probably, and it is worth settling before you choose where records live. The Personal Data Protection Act, Cap 44 of 2022 created the duty, and the [Personal Data Protection Commission](https://pdpc.go.tz) was launched in April 2024 to supervise it. Controllers and processors both register. Your clinic is the controller and the vendor processes on your behalf, so ask them in writing who on their side can open a patient record.

### How does a mobile money payment get recorded?

As a payment method with a provider list you maintain, so it reconciles against a named cashier rather than vanishing into somebody's phone.
